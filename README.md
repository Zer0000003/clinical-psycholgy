Response to Mark Parisi

Main thesis:
Can I learn to face the painful parts of reality without losing the part of myself that still wants connection with other people?

What do you want to work on exactly?
I would like to work on communication, dealing with past relationship trauma, understanding relationships and how I relate to other people, maintaining a healthy balance between work and life, and learning to control my focus and attention in class.

What does success look like for you?
Success would mean being able to participate in society and form relationships without losing who I am in the process. I want to be able to understand other people, communicate myself clearly and appropriately, and adapt to social situations without feeling that I have to abandon or suppress the parts of myself that are important to me.

What are the possible major obstacles standing in your way of counseling success?
I think some of the major obstacles are the effects of past relationship trauma, my own biases and assumptions about people and relationships, and difficulty maintaining the self-discipline necessary to accomplish my goals.

Notes:
There is a persistent pain inside me that I have difficulty understanding. I am trying to accept reality as it is, including the parts of it that are painful, rather than constantly resisting them.

I also recognize that I am drawn toward both sadistic and masochistic experiences. I want to understand that part of myself rather than simply suppressing it or pretending that it is not there. I am interested in understanding why pain, control, vulnerability, and relationships have become psychologically meaningful to me.

For me, there is a tension between wanting to experience and sometimes inflict pain, while simultaneously wanting genuine connection and belonging. I want to understand whether these seemingly contradictory parts of myself can coexist without destroying the relationships and identity that matter to me.

I speculate that some of the persistent pain I experience may be connected to belonging, rejection, past relationships, or feeling disconnected from the world around me. I want counseling to help me distinguish what is actually part of my personality and desires from what may have developed as a response to past experiences.

About me:
I have a dark aesthetic that functions partly as a defense and a way of deterring people who might try to manipulate or attack me. However, underneath that exterior, I consider myself to have a much softer and more vulnerable core. That vulnerability is something I am afraid could be exploited or hurt by other people.
Ultimately, I want to understand how these different parts of myself fit together: my desire for connection, my vulnerability, my attraction to pain and control, my darker exterior, and my desire to understand reality without having to distort myself to fit into it.
